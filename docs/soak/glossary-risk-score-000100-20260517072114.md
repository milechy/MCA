# Ralph Risk Score

The Ralph risk score is a 0–5 scale that measures the likelihood and severity of negative outcomes for any given change or decision. A score of 0 indicates negligible risk, while scores 1 and 2 represent low and moderate risks that can typically be managed with standard precautions. Risk 3 is subdivided into four tiers—3a, 3b, 3c, and 3d—to provide finer granularity for elevated risks that require increasingly specific mitigation strategies before proceeding. Scores of 4 denote high risk, meaning significant potential consequences that demand careful review and executive sign-off. A score of 5 means STOP: the risk is unacceptable under any current circumstances, and work must halt until the situation is fundamentally altered. Teams should always aim to reduce the risk score through mitigation rather than accepting a higher score by default.
